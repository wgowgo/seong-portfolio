Overview

BlindCollective is a Confidential Procurement Network. It detects unpublished future purchase demand, combines that demand without revealing buyer identity or exact amounts, and runs competitive supplier bidding plus collective negotiation.

The blockchain is a verification and state anchor, not a datastore. The durable assets are four layers:

  1. Procurement Intelligence Graph — normalize contracts and spend, infer Future Purchase Intent
  2. Confidential Demand Network — combine demand without identity or exact quantity leakage
  3. Verifiable Market Protocol — commitments, nullifiers, ZK proofs, commit-reveal bids
  4. Procurement Outcome Dataset — accumulate real discount and negotiation outcomes

Design principles

  • Contract text and exact purchase amounts never go on the public chain.
  • Buyer identity and exact quantity stay hidden from other buyers and from suppliers.
  • Operators do not get standing access to raw source data.
  • Demand must be cryptographically verifiable.
  • AI recommends. Legally binding acts pass policy and signatures.

Containers

Consoles

Buyer, Supplier, and Operator consoles are Next.js apps. All writes go through the API Edge.

API Edge

FAPI 2.0 authentication, tenant resolver, WAF, and idempotency. The external contract is OpenAPI 3.2 REST.

Control Plane

Owns organization, verifiable credentials, OPA policy, connections, and Temporal workflows.

Enterprise Edge Agent

A Rust agent that runs inside the buyer network. Connectors are read-only. Raw contracts stay on-prem whenever possible.

Intelligence Plane

Document and contract AI, vendor resolution, forecast, switchability, and the intent compiler. LLM output is not the source of truth.

Confidential Compute

Matching and aggregation run in a TEE. Range and aggregate proofs use Circom or Noir with Groth16.

Market / Chain / Settlement

Market runs RFQ and commit-reveal bidding. Chain writes commitments to registries only. Settlement records payment status and savings.

Decisions

1. Record architecture decisions

Accepted · 2026-08-29

Context

This network mixes privacy, regulation, chain state, and AI authority. If decisions live only in a long spec, review and implementation drift.

Decision

Record architecture decisions as ADRs and attach them to the Structurizr workspace.

Consequences

Model, views, documentation, and decisions can be reviewed in one place.

2. Blockchain is a verification anchor, not a datastore

Accepted · 2026-08-29

Context

Putting legal names, contract text, or exact amounts on a public L2 would break a confidential procurement network.

Decision

On-chain data is limited to organization, intent, pool, bid, and award commitments, nullifier hashes, settlement digests, and audit timestamps. Source documents and exact amounts stay off-chain.

Consequences

Reads verify commitments. Data sovereignty stays in PostgreSQL with tenant KEKs.

3. Confidential matching uses TEE, ZK range proofs, and nullifiers

Accepted · 2026-08-29

Context

Combining demand requires exact amounts, but operators and suppliers must not see those values. The same demand must not be spent twice.

Decision

Exact amounts and organization mappings stay inside a TEE. Public outputs are spend buckets plus Groth16 range and aggregate proofs. Duplicates are blocked with a Poseidon nullifier.

Consequences

The initial proof system is Groth16 on BN254. Verifiers are abstracted so the proving system can change later.

4. Separate AI recommendations from legal authority

Accepted · 2026-08-29

Context

Intent detection and negotiation strategy can be automated. Award, signature, and payment create legal obligations.

Decision

AI produces recommendations and drafts only. Intent publication, pool join, award, contract signature, and payment must pass OPA policy and EIP-712 / ERC-4337 signatures.

Consequences

Enterprises may automate some steps by policy. The default is human approval.
